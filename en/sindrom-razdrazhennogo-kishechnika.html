Irritable bowel syndrome

Abdominal pain, bloating, constipation, diarrhea, or all of them in turn, for months on end. Your tests come back normal, and you're told it's all nerves. This is often what irritable bowel syndrome looks like. It's a real condition, and it can be treated.

What IBS is

In IBS, the connection between the gut and the nervous system is disrupted. The gut becomes more sensitive, contracts differently and reacts more strongly to food, gas and stress. There's no damage to the gut itself, which is why test results look normal.

How it's diagnosed

Using the Rome IV criteria: abdominal pain at least once a week over the past three months that is linked to bowel movements or changes how often you go or what your stool looks like. The ACG guidelines recommend making the diagnosis from these signs, without first running through every possible test.

A few tests are usually enough. With diarrhea, for example, we check for celiac disease and fecal calprotectin so we don't miss inflammation in the bowel. A colonoscopy is needed if there are warning signs:

  • blood in your stool;
  • you're losing weight without trying;
  • your blood tests show iron deficiency anemia;
  • pain or diarrhea wakes you up at night;
  • you have a fever that won't go away;
  • a close relative has had bowel cancer, Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis or celiac disease;
  • your symptoms started after age 45: from that age, everyone is advised to be screened for bowel cancer.

What helps

I tailor treatment to your type of IBS: with constipation, with diarrhea, or mixed. Research supports soluble fiber (psyllium), a short low-FODMAP diet followed by bringing foods back, peppermint oil, medications that make the gut less sensitive, and gut-directed psychotherapy. We usually combine two or three approaches and see what works for you.

Frequently asked questions

Is IBS treatable?+

Yes. Most patients get a noticeable reduction in their symptoms. Sometimes it takes time and a few attempts, so we go step by step.

Is it dangerous?+

IBS doesn't turn into cancer or other serious diseases. But if there are warning signs, you need tests so that another condition isn't missed.

Will probiotics help?+

The ACG guidelines advise against probiotics for IBS: there's no convincing evidence that they help.

Can we work on this online?+

Yes. Online, I go through your symptoms, diet and test results in detail and give you a written plan.
